0. Construction sequence ANCHORwhat made the calibrated graph work
StepDo this before drawingReject when
1. business anchorName the durable record or transaction that gives the graph a center: invoice, support case, order, message, payment ledger.The center is a run, suite, helper, report, XML, Extent or generic "flow".
2. actor and intentName who wants the outcome and why the product should preserve or expose it.The first visible node is a technical action or test fixture.
3. owned objectHighlight the primary owned business object with the strongest visual emphasis.All nodes look equally important or the graph has no primary object.
4. relationship labelsLabel arrows with business verbs such as owns, anchors, classifies, captures, submits, confirms, reopens, awaits.Arrows are only chronology or unlabeled plumbing.
5. frontierRender the next object as dashed/capped and explicitly not owned.The graph ends with success even though the business lifecycle is still open.
6. proof boundarySend run IDs, XML, Extent, diff, state and method names to Proof Mapping, Run Audit or a collapsed proof strip.Proof artifacts become business nodes.
Start from the business object, not from the evidence. Evidence proves the map; it is not the map.

4. Bad smells INVALIDwhen you cool the graph
  • If the reader remembers run_03, repair, or reentry before the business lane, the graph is too procedural.
  • If the macro-map of the application fits in one corner, and the overlay occupies the whole, the report is package-first.
  • If the nodes already contain the proof, the diff and the explanation, the graph is trying to do too much.
  • If the legend repeats the entire graph, it is no longer a legend, but a poorly disguised essay.
